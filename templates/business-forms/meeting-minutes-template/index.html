Meeting minutes are the written record of what a meeting decided and who will do what next. This meeting minutes template gives you one page for the meeting details, who attended, the discussion and decision for each agenda item, an action items table and the next meeting date. It comes filled with a sample monthly review so you can see how much to write, then you replace it with your own notes.

How to use this meeting minutes template

  1. Download the Word file and open it in Microsoft Word, Google Docs or LibreOffice Writer.
  2. Replace the organisation name, meeting number, date, time and venue at the top.
  3. Write the chairperson, the person taking minutes, who was present and who sent apologies.
  4. For each agenda item, note who led it, the main points in one or two lines and the decision taken.
  5. List every action item with one owner and a due date. Leave the status as Open.
  6. Add the next meeting date and topics, then send the minutes to everyone within a day or two.
  7. After the chairperson approves the minutes, sign them and save a PDF copy for your records.

What to include in meeting minutes

Good minutes cover the basics: the group name, meeting date, time and place, who chaired, who attended and who was absent. They say whether the previous minutes and the agenda were approved. For each agenda item they record the topic, who presented it, the decision and who is responsible, with deadlines. They end with the time the meeting closed and the next meeting details.

This template has a place for each of these. Minutes do not need to repeat every word that was said. Write the decision and the reason in short, plain sentences.

What is inside the file

An A4 page with a header for the meeting title and details, three shaded boxes for the chair, members present and apologies, a five column discussion table (#, Agenda item, Led by, Key points discussed, Decision), an action items table with owner, due date and status, a next meeting section and three signature lines. Add or remove table rows as you need.

Formal meetings have their own rules

Board meetings, society meetings and general meetings of a company often have rules about notice, quorum, how minutes are signed and how long they must be kept. These rules depend on the law that applies to your organisation and on its own constitution or bye-laws. Use this template for team and office meetings, and check the official rules or ask your company secretary before using it for statutory minutes.

Tips for best results

  • Take rough notes during the meeting and write the clean minutes the same day.
  • Write decisions in the past tense, like Agreed to move go-live to 1 January.
  • Give every action item one owner, even when two people help.
  • Start the next meeting by checking the open action items from the last minutes.
  • Keep a numbered series, like MOM-2026-11, so minutes are easy to find later.

Frequently asked questions

Who should write the meeting minutes?

Usually a secretary, a team member or someone chosen at the start of the meeting. It helps if the minute taker is not also leading the discussion.

What is the difference between an agenda and minutes?

An agenda is the list of topics sent before the meeting. Minutes are the record made during and after the meeting of what was discussed, decided and assigned.

Should minutes record who said what?

For most office meetings, no. Record the main points, the decision and the action owners. Some formal meetings need votes or objections recorded, so follow your organisation's rules.

Can I use this template for online meetings?

Yes. Type the video call link in the Venue line and list the people who joined online under Present.

How do I share the minutes?

Save the Word file as PDF and email it, or upload it to your shared folder. A PDF keeps the layout the same on every phone and computer.
