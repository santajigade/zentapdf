This expense tracker in Excel has two sheets. On the Expenses sheet you add one line for each payment, and the Summary sheet totals what you spent in any month by category and by payment mode, next to a budget you set. The formulas are plain SUMIFS, so the file works in Excel, Google Sheets and LibreOffice with no macros.

How to use this expense tracker excel

  1. Download the Excel file and open it in Microsoft Excel, Google Sheets or LibreOffice Calc.
  2. On the Summary sheet, change the categories and monthly budget to suit you. Leave a row blank if you need fewer.
  3. Go to the Expenses sheet and delete the sample lines.
  4. Each time you pay for something, add the date, what it was for, the amount and pick a category and payment mode from the drop-downs.
  5. Open the Summary sheet and type any date in the Month cell to see that month's spending, money left and % used.
  6. At month end, save the Summary sheet as PDF if you want a record to share or keep.

What is inside the file

Expenses sheet: 60 rows with Date, What you paid for, Category, Paid by, Amount and Notes. The header row stays in view as you scroll and repeats on every printed page. A small line at the top counts your entries and their total.

Summary sheet: a Month cell, three big figures for budget, spent and left, a table of 12 categories with budget, spent, left and % used, and a Paid by table that splits the month by Cash, UPI, cards and net banking. Categories over budget turn red.

How the monthly totals are calculated

The Spent column uses SUMIFS. It adds up the Amount column only for lines where the category matches and the date falls between the first and last day of the chosen month. That means you can keep several months of entries on one sheet and still see each month on its own by changing a single date.

Because the match is exact, an entry typed as "grocery" will not count under Groceries. The drop-down list stops this, and it reads the category names from the Summary sheet, so renaming a category there updates the list too.

Getting real value from tracking

Most people are surprised by small spends, such as food delivery, short rides and subscriptions, rather than big bills. Logging every payment for a full month, then comparing spent with budget, shows where the money really goes. Set next month's budgets from what you actually spent, not what you hoped to spend.

Tips for best results

  • Add expenses the same day, or copy them from your bank or UPI app once a week.
  • Use the Notes column for details you may need later, like a bill number or who you paid.
  • For more than 60 lines, insert rows above the last row of the log so the Summary formulas include them.
  • Keep one file per year and start a fresh copy each January.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use this expense tracker in Google Sheets?

Yes. Upload the file to Google Drive and open it with Google Sheets. The drop-downs, SUMIFS formulas and red highlights keep working.

Can I track more than one month?

Yes. Keep adding lines on the Expenses sheet. The Summary sheet shows only the month in its Month cell, so change that date to see any other month.

How do I add or rename a category?

Type over a category name on the Summary sheet, or fill one of the rows you are not using. The Category drop-down on the Expenses sheet reads that list, so it updates too.

Why is an expense missing from the summary?

Check that its date is a real date in the chosen month and that its category matches a name on the Summary sheet exactly. Picking from the drop-down avoids spelling differences.

Can I use it for business expenses?

Yes, for simple tracking. Rename the categories to things like travel, office supplies and software. For tax claims, keep the bills and follow your accountant's advice and the official rules where you live.
