A travel itinerary keeps the whole trip in one place: where you are going, where you are staying, every booking with its confirmation number, and what you plan to do each day. This template fits all of that on one printable A4 page. The Excel version numbers the days from your travel dates and adds up bookings and daily spends against your trip budget.

How to use this travel itinerary template

  1. Download the PDF to print, the Word file to type in, or the Excel file if you want dates and totals worked out.
  2. Fill in the trip details at the top: destination, travel dates, travellers and a contact at home.
  3. Add each booking as you make it, with the date, time, confirmation or PNR number and the cost.
  4. Plan each day in three parts, morning, afternoon and evening, and note where you are staying that night.
  5. Go through the documents list a day or two before you leave and tick what you have packed.
  6. In Excel, type the start and end dates on the Itinerary sheet. A row with its day number and date appears for each day of the trip, up to 14 days.
  7. On the Bookings sheet, type your trip budget and each booking, then pick Yes in Paid? once you have paid for it.

What is in the file

The printable page has a trip details box, a bookings table with rows labelled for travel out, stay, local transport, activity, travel back and insurance, a seven-day plan split into morning, afternoon and evening, and a checklist of documents to carry.

The Excel file has two sheets. Itinerary works out the number of nights from your dates and lists each day with its date, place, plan, stay and expected spend. Bookings has room for 20 bookings, with a type drop-down and a Paid? column. The summary at the top adds up bookings and daily spends, shows what you have paid and what is still to pay, and turns red if the plan goes over budget.

What to put in an itinerary

Write down anything you would struggle to find on a weak phone signal: hotel addresses and phone numbers, check-in and check-out times, flight or train numbers, pickup points and confirmation codes. Note the opening days of places you want to see, because many museums and monuments close one day a week. Leave some free time each day, since a plan that fills every hour usually falls behind.

Documents and travel rules

Passport, visa, ID and health entry rules depend on where you are going and where you are from, and they change. Check the official website of the destination country or your airline before you book, and again before you travel. For trips inside India, airlines and hotels usually ask for a valid photo ID, so keep one handy for every traveller.

Tips for best results

  • Save the itinerary as a PDF on your phone and share it with someone at home.
  • Write times in the local time of each place, especially for flights that cross time zones.
  • Keep the first and last day light, because travel days almost always take longer than expected.

Frequently asked questions

How many days does the Excel itinerary handle?

Up to 14 days. Rows after your end date stay blank. For a longer trip, insert rows inside the day table and copy the Day and Date formulas down.

Can I use it for a business trip?

Yes. Use the bookings table for flights, hotels and cabs, and the day plan for meetings. With the costs in one place, an expense claim is easier to fill in afterwards.

Does it work for group trips?

Yes. List all travellers in the trip details, and use the Details column to show who each booking is for.

What does Bookings still to pay show?

The total cost of your bookings minus the ones marked Yes in the Paid? column. It does not include daily spends, which you pay as you go.

Can I open it in Google Sheets?

Yes. Upload the file to Google Drive and open it with Google Sheets. The dates, drop-downs and totals work the same way.
