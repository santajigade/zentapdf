This weekly planner template puts your whole week on one A4 landscape page. Each day has its own column for appointments and tasks, and a strip along the bottom holds your top three priorities, a to-do list, a small habit tracker and notes. Print it every Sunday or fill it on screen in Word.

How to use this weekly planner template

  1. Download the PDF to print right away, or the Word file if you want to type in it or change the headings.
  2. Print in landscape on A4 or Letter paper, at actual size.
  3. Write the dates for the week at the top, starting with Monday.
  4. Choose your top three priorities for the week before you fill the day columns.
  5. Add fixed appointments to each day first, then fit tasks around them.
  6. At the end of the week, tick off habits, carry unfinished tasks to next week and keep the sheet in a file.

Why plan by the week

A daily list shows only today, so big tasks keep getting pushed. A monthly calendar shows dates but has no room for tasks. A weekly view sits in between: you can see every appointment and deadline for seven days and still have space to plan the work around them.

Many people plan on Sunday evening or Monday morning. Ten minutes is enough to look at the week ahead, choose what matters most and spread the work across the days.

How to use each part of the planner

Day columns: write timed appointments at the top of each column with the time, then tasks below. Keep each day realistic, with no more than three to five important tasks. Top three priorities: the outcomes that would make the week a success, such as finishing a report or revising two chapters.

To-do: tasks for the week that have no fixed day yet. Move them into a day column when you know when you will do them. Habit tracker: five rows to tick each day, for example water, exercise, reading, sleep by 11 pm or no phone after dinner. Notes: ideas, phone numbers and anything you want to remember.

Printing and reusing it

The planner is designed for A4 landscape and prints with clean margins on home printers. To make a small booklet, print several weeks and staple them, or punch holes and keep them in a ring binder. If you prefer digital planning, open the Word file on your computer, type in each box and save a copy for every week.

Tips for best results

  • Plan the week at the same time every week so it becomes a habit.
  • Write appointments in pen and tasks in pencil, so you can move tasks without a mess.
  • Leave one or two empty spaces each day for surprises.
  • Colour code by area of life, for example blue for work, green for health and orange for family.
  • Review last week's sheet before you plan the new one, and carry over anything unfinished.

Frequently asked questions

What paper size does this planner use?

A4 landscape. It also prints on US Letter if you choose Fit to page in the print settings.

Can I start the week on Sunday?

Yes. Open the Word file and change the day names in the table header, starting with Sunday, then save or print.

Can I fill it on my phone?

You can open the PDF on your phone and type in it with the free Edit PDF tool on ZentaPDF, but the planner is easiest to use printed or on a computer.

How do I print a whole month of weekly planners?

Print the PDF four or five times, or use the copies setting in your print dialog. To keep a digital set, use Merge PDF to join several copies into one file.
